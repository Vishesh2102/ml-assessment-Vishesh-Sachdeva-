# Part B: Business Case Analysis — Promotion Effectiveness

---

## B1. Problem Formulation

### (a) Formulation
* **Target Variable:** `items_sold` (Continuous numerical value).
* **Input Features:** Store location type, store size, competition density, promotion type, and calendar features (**weekend**, **festival**, **month**).
* **Type of ML Problem:** **Supervised Regression**.
* **Justification:** The goal is to predict a specific quantity of goods sold. Since the output is continuous, regression is the appropriate choice. This allows the business to simulate all five promotion types for a given store and select the one that maximizes predicted sales volume.

### (b) Target Variable Reliability
* **Revenue vs. Volume:** Revenue is a "noisy" metric often distorted by price fluctuations, deep discounting, and product mix. A promotion might look successful in revenue terms while failing to increase actual customer engagement.
* **Why Items Sold:** It directly reflects customer demand and footfall generated by the marketing lever.
* **Broader Principle:** This illustrates **Proximal Target Selection**. Choosing a target that is closely aligned with the action being tested (the promotion) improves model reliability and simplifies the decision-making process.

### (c) Alternative Modelling Strategy
* **Proposed Approach:** **Hybrid Modelling** (Segmentation + Interaction).
* **Methodology:** * Develop **interaction features** (e.g., `promotion_type` × `location_type`) to capture how different areas respond to specific offers.
    * Segment stores by location or performance clusters to build specialized sub-models.
* **Justification:** A single global model often averages out local nuances (like rural price sensitivity), while fully separate models lose the benefit of shared patterns across the chain. A hybrid approach captures location-specific behavior while maintaining overall predictive power.

## B2. Data and EDA Strategy

### (a) Table Joins and Grain
* **Base Table:** Calendar Table (serves as the "spine" to ensure all dates are represented).
* **Joins:** * **Transactions + Store** via `store_id`.
    * **Transactions + Promotions** via date/promotion ID.
* **Grain:** One row per **Store-Day**.
* **Aggregations:** * Sum of `items_sold` per store per day.
    * Apply **One-Hot Encoding** to categorical features (location, promotion type).

### (b) EDA Strategy
* **Correlation Heatmap:** To identify multicollinearity and determine which features (like store size or competition) have the strongest linear relationship with sales.
* **Promotion-wise Boxplots:** To visualize the median "lift" and variability across the five promotion types.
* **Time-Series Analysis:** To isolate natural seasonality (festivals, weekends) from true promotional impact.
* **Outlier Detection:** To identify unusual sales spikes that may indicate data errors or unique local events requiring special handling.
* **Impact:** EDA findings guide feature engineering, such as deciding whether to scale features or create new holiday-related variables.

### (c) Handling Imbalance
* **Problem:** If 80% of data has no promotion, the model will develop a strong bias toward "business-as-usual" scenarios, potentially ignoring the features that drive promotional lift.
* **Solutions:** * **Oversampling** promotion periods during the training phase.
    * **Cost-Sensitive Learning**, assigning a higher weight to errors occurring on promotional days.
* **Goal:** To ensure the model is highly sensitive to the specific features that differentiate a "promo day" from a standard sales day.

## B3. Model Evaluation and Deployment

### (a) Train-Test Split and Metrics
* **Split Strategy:** **Temporal (Chronological) Split**.
* **Why not Random:** Shuffling time-series data leads to **Data Leakage**, where the model "cheats" by using future trends to predict past events—an impossible feat in production.
* **Metrics:** * **MAE (Mean Absolute Error):** Provides the average prediction error in physical units, making it easy for store managers to understand.
    * **RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error):** Penalizes large misses more heavily, which is vital for avoiding critical stockouts.
    * **MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error):** Allows for performance comparison across stores of varying sizes.

### (b) Feature Importance & Store-Level Insights
* **Approach:** Use **SHAP values** or Global Feature Importance to explain model behavior.
* **Scenario Explanation:**
    * **December:** The model identifies high natural demand due to the "Festival" flag; thus, it recommends a **Loyalty Bonus** to retain margin without unnecessary discounting.
    * **March:** In the absence of seasonal triggers, the model identifies a "demand gap" and recommends a **Flat Discount** to artificially stimulate volume.
* **Communication:** Use visual charts to show the marketing team how the calendar (natural demand) and promotions (artificial demand) interact to drive the final prediction.

### (c) Deployment and Monitoring
* **Model Deployment:** Serialize the full pipeline (StandardScaler + Regressor) using `joblib` for consistency.
* **Monthly Process:** 1. Input the upcoming month's parameters.
    2. Run the model 5 times (once for each promotion) per store.
    3. Output the recommendation that maximizes predicted `items_sold`.
* **Monitoring:** * Track **Data Drift** (changes in input distributions) and **Concept Drift** (changes in the relationship between features and sales).
    * **Retraining Trigger:** If the MAE consistently exceeds a predefined threshold for two months, trigger a manual review and retraining session.
